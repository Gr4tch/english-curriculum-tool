### Section Reading Passage (By Subheading)

#### 1. PEACE REJECTED (p. 206)
**[¶1]** During the summer of 1775, the Continental Congress made moves toward both peace and war. In an attempt to restore peace with Britain, delegates sent the king a petition. They asked him to resolve the conflict between the colonies and Parliament. Instead, he proclaimed that the colonies were in "an open and avowed rebellion." At the same time, Congress sent an army north to invade Canada and take on the British forces there.

**[¶2]** This proved to be a poor decision. The Canada campaign was long, bloody, and unsuccessful. British troops successfully pushed the colonial army back into New York. In December 1775, Parliament decided to punish the colonies by cutting off all trade. Then in January 1776, King George signed a treaty with Germany that allowed him to hire more than 20,000 German soldiers and send them to the colonies.

**[¶3]** These soldiers for hire were known as Hessians , and they had a reputation as fierce fighters. For many Americans, these actions proved that the British government intended to crush the colonies both militarily and economically, at any cost.

#### 2. TIME TO PART (p. 206)
**[¶1]** Meanwhile, the colonial army was meeting with greater success in Boston. George Washington had arrived there during the summer of 1775, determined to drive the British from the city. The following winter, he sent troops to drag the heavy guns from Fort Ticonderoga and install them on high ground overlooking Boston.

**[¶2]** In the spring of 1776, under the threat of the looming artillery, the British withdrew from the city. They sailed north to Canada in preparation for an eventual invasion of New York City. More than a thousand Loyalists left with the British because they feared they would not be safe from angry Patriots if they stayed in Boston.

**[¶3]** Alarmed by the colonists' show of force in Boston, the British government sent a massive force to put down the revolt. Nearly 400 ships set sail from England carrying 32,000 troops, including at least 12,000 Hessians. The soldiers would join the British troops already gathering in Canada for an attack on New York City.

**[¶4]** The warships would attack seaports and disrupt colonial shipping. Congress has always had a difficult job, making decisions that will impact a large number of people. In 1775, the Second Continental Congress faced a hard choice: pursue peace or prepare for war. **[¶7]** In January 1776, Thomas Paine published a pamphlet called Common Sense to argue the case for independence. In the pamphlet, Paine made several practical arguments for independence. He claimed that France and Spain would only aid the colonies if they broke with Britain. He also pointed out that European countries would continue to purchase American exports if the colonies declared independence. "'Tis time to part," he urged his readers.
